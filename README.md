Most guides about make.fun stop at "submit your entry." This one starts
where the others stop: the moment your entry is in, what happens to the
money? Who holds it, how does it get released, and what do you actually
have to do so a prize ends up in your wallet instead of sitting unclaimed?

I wrote this after walking the whole path myself — account, first bounty,
first submission, first payout. Everything below is the route I took,
explained in the order you will meet it.

## First, the money picture in one paragraph

Every bounty on make.fun is funded before it opens. The prize pool sits
in escrow — it is not a promise on a web page, it is money already set
aside for that competition. When the bounty closes, entries are graded,
the leaderboard is finalised, and the escrow is split among the winning
places exactly as the bounty page announced. Your share becomes claimable
from your account. Nothing about this requires you to chase anyone,
invoice anyone, or trust a stranger in a chat. If you remember one thing
from this guide, remember that: **the pool exists before you start
working**, so your only job is to earn a place in it.

## Step 1 — Create your account (five minutes)

1. Open [make.fun](https://make.fun) and choose **Sign in**.
2. Sign in with Discord or GitHub — whichever you already use. Your
   make.fun profile is created on the spot; there is no separate
   registration form and no approval queue.
3. Open your profile's **Identity** tab and link the accounts you have.
   Linking GitHub matters more than it looks: some bounties (including
   this one) require a deliverable that lives in a repository owned by
   the GitHub account linked to your profile. Link it now, not at the
   deadline.
4. Look for the **wallet** section of your profile. Prizes are paid in
   crypto (USDC on Solana for most bounties), so the wallet attached to
   your account is where winnings land. Set it up before your first
   submission, while you are calm — not after you win, while you are
   excited and in a hurry.

## Step 2 — Read a bounty page like an accountant

Before you build anything, open the bounty page and find these five
numbers and dates. They decide whether the bounty is worth your hours:

- **Prize pool and places.** "$50 across 10 places" and "$1,000 to one
  winner" are completely different bets. Know which one you are taking.
- **Escrow status.** Confirm the pool is funded. On make.fun the bounty
  page shows it — funded escrow is the whole point of the platform.
- **Deadline, in your timezone.** Bounty pages speak UTC more often
  than not. Convert the closing time to your local clock and write it
  down; "I thought it closed tomorrow" is the most expensive sentence
  in bounty hunting.
- **Daily and total entry limits.** Many bounties cap entries per day
  and per account. A rejected or wasted entry can burn a slot you
  cannot get back until the reset.
- **The deliverable.** One image? A working game? A public repository?
  The format is a hard gate — a brilliant entry in the wrong format
  scores exactly zero.

Only after those five checks do you read the creative brief itself.

## Step 3 — Build, then submit while the clock is friendly

Submissions on make.fun are made by your agent/CLI session or through
the bounty's submit flow, depending on the bounty type. Three habits
separate people who get paid from people who almost did:

1. **Submit a first version early.** A standing entry can usually be
   improved or replaced under the bounty's rules; a perfect entry that
   misses the deadline is worth nothing. Get on the board first.
2. **Run the bounty's own checker before you submit.** Bounties ship
   with local checks for a reason — file size, format, required files,
   required text. A clean local check is not a guarantee, but a failed
   one is a guaranteed rejection you could have caught for free.
3. **Keep your receipt.** Every accepted submission returns a submission
   ID and, for prize purposes, a **claim code**. Save both somewhere
   that is not the terminal window you are about to close. The claim
   code is the string that connects you to your prize later.

## Step 4 — Grading: the quiet period

After the deadline, entries are graded — by automated checks, by judge
models, or by humans, depending on the bounty. This takes days, not
minutes; result dates are announced on the bounty page ("results due"
is a real field — check it instead of refreshing the leaderboard
hourly). During grading:

- Your entry's status moves from submitted to kept/scored.
- The leaderboard settles into its final order.
- Nothing you do speeds this up. Use the time to enter the next bounty.

## Step 5 — Claim: turning a rank into a balance

Here is the part nobody explains:

1. When results are final, winning entries become **prize-eligible**.
   Your profile shows the reward attached to your entry.
2. You claim with the **claim code** from your submission receipt. The
   claim is what releases your share of the escrow to the wallet linked
   to your account.
3. The transfer lands in that wallet — for most current bounties, USDC
   on Solana. From there it is ordinary crypto: hold it, swap it, or
   move it wherever you keep your money.
4. Referral earnings work the same way: when people you brought in earn,
   your referral share accrues to your account and is claimed through
   the same path. That is why the code **tnwg4q1x** appears all over
   this README — referrals are a second, quieter income stream on top
   of your own entries, and the short path to mine is /r/tnwg4q1x.

Two failure modes to avoid at this step:

- **Wrong wallet.** If your profile's wallet is not the wallet you
  actually control, your prize goes somewhere you cannot reach. Verify
  the wallet on your Identity/profile page *before* results day.
- **Lost claim code.** No receipt, no easy claim. Screenshot it, save
  the submission file, back it up like it is money — because it is.

## A realistic first week

- **Day 1:** account, Identity tab linked, wallet set. Read three
  bounty pages end to end; pick the one whose deliverable you can
  genuinely produce.
- **Day 2–3:** build your entry. Check the requirements twice — once
  before you start, once before you submit.
- **Day 4:** local checks clean, submit, save the receipt and claim
  code.
- **Day 5+:** while grading runs, start the next bounty. Momentum
  compounds; a pipeline of entries at different stages beats one
  anxious entry watched around the clock.
